# Evidence base for the Smoke-Free Path
1. Kaushik P, Sharma N, Singh M, Aggarwal P. Tobacco Cessation Centres: Vital Cog in the Fight against Tobacco Epidemic Preparedness towards a future pandemic. Healthline 2023;14(3):244-247. Finding used: WHO and India's MoHFW established official tobacco cessation clinics that counsel people trying to quit and monitor their progress, alone or combined with medication. Both smokers and smokeless-tobacco users benefit substantially from quitting; quitting lowers the risk of respiratory disease, delays its onset and slows chronic lung disease progression.
2. Kaushik P, Sharma N, Aggarwal P, Singh M, Ojha A. Evaluation of Tobacco Cessation Centres under the National Tobacco Control Programme in Uttarakhand. Indian J Community Health 2025;37(5):842-847. Findings used: TCC services are counselling, nicotine replacement therapy, follow-up and IEC (information, education, communication) material. In the study year 24,660 tobacco users were counselled; only 1,266 (about 5%) were under regular follow-up and 241 quit. Design implication for us: FOLLOW-UP is the weak link, so the app's reminders, daily check-ins and re-engagement are its main contribution.
3. Behavioural interventions for tobacco cessation in India: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Family Med Prim Care 2023. Finding used: 16 studies (9,613 participants), behavioural interventions were associated with a higher likelihood of quitting (relative risk roughly 1.7 to 2.0); brief behavioural interventions can be cost-effective additions to health services.
